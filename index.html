Every chapter closes with a pass/fail checklist. You only move on — and only start spending on video generation — when the cheap stage is provably right. The worked example throughout is the film above, The Last Lighthouse, with its real shot board filled in.

CH 1Tools & cost control▾

The multi-model workflow (text → image → video), what each stage should cost, and where to check before you spend anything on generation.

CH 2Idea to script▾

Turning one sentence of an idea into a story outline and a scene-by-scene script that a generation tool can actually follow.

CH 3Storyboarding▾

Writing a shot list with framing, action and duration per shot — the document that keeps a 12-shot film coherent instead of random.

CH 4Character & scene consistency▾

Reference sheets, fixed character anchors and style anchors: how the same keeper and the same daughter appear in all 12 shots of the demo film.

Keyframes first, hardest shot first, and a hard budget of 3 attempts per shot — the rule that kept the demo film at zero wasted generations.

Assembly, trims, subtitles, loudness (−14 LUFS) and the export settings for Shorts, TikTok and Reels.
