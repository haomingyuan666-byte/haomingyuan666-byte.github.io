Why Are 1688 Prices So Low? What One Factory Quote Revealed

November 15, 2026

If you are wondering “why are 1688 prices so low,” check which configuration the listing price buys. The cheapest selectable option may be an accessory or a smaller model. In Hao Studio's inquiry for a 3.2L stainless steel pet fountain, the card displayed ¥29.80. The confirmed quote for the complete unit was ¥108 at 200 units or ¥106 at 500 units.

Read the displayed price as a prompt to identify the configuration and order quantity. Then ask for a written per-unit quote for the exact model. You can use that quote to compare like with like and build a cost estimate. Keep different configurations and unresolved offers visible in your notes so their numbers do not get mixed into the budget.

Find what the cheapest selectable option buys

Open the product options and look at the configuration attached to the initial price. A product image can show the full fountain while the lowest option refers to a component or a smaller version. Check the option name, capacity, material, and included items side by side. For a supplier inquiry, describe the configuration in words and include the variant reference if the listing gives you one. A screenshot of the main product image alone can leave the seller guessing which selection you mean.

In the Hao Studio inquiry, the advertised ¥29.80 applied to an accessory or smaller-capacity option. This is a useful distinction for a buyer who needs a complete fountain: both prices can belong to the same listing while answering different questions. The card price helps you find the catalog entry. The unit quote answers what the complete fountain costs at your intended quantity, provided the seller confirms the configuration in writing.

Compare prices by what the evidence establishes

Hao Studio contacted 15 sellers and shortlisted 9. Quotes for the complete unit fell in a ¥100–153 factory price band. The final confirmed tiers were ¥108 and ¥106, depending on quantity. The inquiry also encountered a ¥65 quote that remained unverified and a ¥61 card price whose unit scope could not be confirmed. Keep those entries in a separate status in the comparison so they do not act as the low end of the verified range.

Otherwise an old estimate can get passed around with no visible record of how the dollar figures were produced.

Ask every seller the same question

Write an inquiry that identifies the exact product and asks for unit pricing at the quantities you are prepared to buy. In this case, capacity and stainless steel construction helped distinguish the intended model. Ask the seller to say plainly whether the quote is for the complete unit and to list what is included. Also ask how it is packed and which costs are quoted separately. If they answer a different quantity or configuration, record that deviation and ask them to price the original request too.

Store each answer with the exact variant named in the message. A useful quote sheet includes the seller reference, option, confirmed scope, quantity, unit price, and date. Add a column for the current evidence status. A product card, a chat response containing a number, and an itemized quote all mean different things during review. Keeping the message with the row lets someone verify a decision without reopening the sourcing conversation from scratch.

Some sellers responded to follow-up questions with an emoji or a contact card. In the quote sheet, that still leaves the scope or order terms unanswered. Ask for the missing item directly, and keep the entry open if a substantive response has yet to arrive. A seller may be available through another channel. Record what was actually confirmed before assigning a price to the shortlist, regardless of where the reply came in.

Check whether repeated specifications affect the comparison

Multiple stores listed the same dimensions and weight, 23 × 12 × 23 cm and 1100 g. These matching details raised the possibility that the listings shared a source or distribution channel. Use them as a reason to check the relationship. They may also come from reused catalog material. Ask whose specification is being quoted, whether the dimensions describe the item or the package, and whether the seller can confirm the version they would actually supply.

This matters when the purpose of the comparison is to find a backup source. Matching photos, dimensions, and weights can make several offers feel independent even when they depend on the same inventory. Find out who supplies the product, who takes responsibility for the order, and who can answer configuration questions. A seller's role and the support they can provide are useful inputs to the decision alongside the unit price.

Put the verified tier into an order budget

Choose the quantity you would actually buy and carry the matching confirmed unit price into the model. The ¥106 tier belongs to the 500-unit order and the ¥108 tier belongs to the 200-unit order. Compare the full commitment at each quantity, including how much stock you can take and where it will go. A lower unit price can still require a larger cash commitment. Leave freight, inspection, or any other separately quoted cost as its own line and label estimates as such.

Ask for an updated written quote before approving an order if the configuration, quantity, packaging, or delivery terms change. Keep the earlier quote for reference, and link the budget to the current one. When a lower price appears later, ask the seller to explain the scope in the same terms that supported the original estimate. If they can confirm equivalent terms, you have comparable evidence. If important details remain unanswered, the new price remains an open line in the comparison.

Keep sample and production pricing in separate rows

Ask whether a sample would use the same materials and accessories as the production unit. Save the sample quote with its shipping terms and keep the bulk quote linked to its own quantity tier. If the sample configuration differs, describe what needs to be checked on the production version before the buyer approves the order. When sharing the budget, show the basis for the per-unit estimate next to the amount. That makes it possible to update a single line if packaging changes without losing the evidence that supported the original calculation.

Keep a reusable record of how you got the price

Save the listing selection, the configuration you requested, and the seller's reply together. The record should show which prices came from the card, which came from the conversation, and which were confirmed for the whole product. That makes the next comparison faster when the listing changes or someone else takes over purchasing.
