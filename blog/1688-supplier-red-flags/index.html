1688 Supplier Red Flags to Check Before You Shortlist a Seller

November 15, 2026

The 1688 supplier red flags worth acting on are the ones that leave a buying decision unsupported. An emoji in place of a quote, a low price with no confirmed configuration, and identical specifications across several shops can each leave you with a different unanswered question. Identify the missing evidence before you decide whether the seller belongs on your shortlist.

In Hao Studio's inquiry for a 3.2L stainless steel pet fountain, some sellers replied only with an emoji or a contact card, one ¥65 quote could not be verified, and several shops published identical dimensions and weight. Use a follow-up that asks for a specific piece of evidence, record the reply, and shortlist the sellers whose answers cover the product, unit price, and order terms you need.

Start with a screening request everyone can answer

Write a brief containing the exact product, the configuration you want, and the quantity you are considering. Give the seller a short list of questions that can be answered in the message. Ask what is included in the unit, what the price is for that quantity, and who would handle the order. If you only send a product photo and ask for the best price, a seller has room to choose the variant and the quantity behind the number. You then have no consistent basis for the comparison.

Track the listing, your question, and the actual response together. Use a buyer-facing reference for each supplier so people reviewing the shortlist can trace a claim to a message. Add a place to record the decision and its reason. This helps when the cheapest reply is also the least specific. A low figure and an unanswered scope question should both remain visible to the person approving the purchase.

The reply gives you a contact instead of an answer

A contact card gives you another route to a sales representative. An emoji can acknowledge a message. Neither response tells you whether the quoted unit includes the components you need. If you change channels, carry the same product request into the new conversation and ask for a written confirmation you can retain in your file. Record the identity of the person who gave the answer and the seller account they represent.

Ask for one concrete missing item first. For example, ask which capacity is covered by the unit price, or whether the quote covers the complete unit. A seller who answers clearly has moved the inquiry forward even if other conditions still need review. If a follow-up still yields only contact details or acknowledgments, mark the specific unanswered field. You can park that entry while continuing with sellers who have supplied enough detail to compare. The reason for the decision should be visible in your log.

A price looks better than the other offers but lacks scope

In the fountain inquiry, the complete-unit factory quotes fell between ¥100 and ¥153. The ¥65 quote had no verification and the ¥61 product card could not be confirmed as the complete model. The card showing ¥29.80 applied to an accessory or smaller version. These observations give the buyer a concrete way to frame a follow-up. Ask the seller to link the number to the exact configuration and order quantity, then state what is included.

If the seller confirms the scope, still ask which terms explain the difference. The quote may depend on packaging, minimum quantity, or another condition that changes the total commitment. Record that condition alongside the price so the question can be revisited if your order changes. If the seller supplies no confirmation, keep the number in the inquiry record as unresolved. A shortlist intended for purchasing needs prices that someone can trace to the product they expect to receive.

Several shops repeat the same product specifications

Exact matches in both dimensions and weight can be worth checking, especially if the stores also describe the product in the same way. In this inquiry, the repeated 23 × 12 × 23 cm and 1100 g specifications pointed to possible common sourcing. Shared catalog copy can produce the same pattern. Treat the match as a reason to ask about supply, inventory, and who will answer for the order if the delivered item differs from the quote.

Ask the seller to confirm which physical configuration the dimensions refer to. Item dimensions and packaged dimensions can differ, and that difference matters for identifying the model and estimating freight. If you need independent backups, ask where the sellers source their stock and how they would handle availability changes. Keep a shared-source note until you can support an independence claim. If independent supply is less important to your order, the same conversation can help you compare service and accountability.

Revisit the shortlist after the follow-up

Hao Studio contacted 15 sellers and shortlisted 9. The counts describe the scope of this inquiry. Build your own shortlist around the evidence needed for your product and purchase, and update it as replies arrive. A seller can be ready for a sample request while still missing a freight estimate for a bulk order. Record which decision the available evidence supports, then list what remains open for the next step.

Give each pending seller a clear question. “Please confirm the complete unit at our requested quantity and list what ships in the carton” is easier to review than “please send more information.” Save the answer with its date. If the seller changes the quoted configuration or quantity, retain the earlier response and update the comparison only after you can tell what changed. This makes it possible to revisit any offer that looked attractive on the first pass.

Make the sample handoff a separate decision

Before paying for a sample, write down what the seller says will arrive and who will arrange the shipment. If the purchasing decision depends on a particular fitting or accessory, list it among the items to confirm when the sample arrives. Reuse the same configuration description in a later purchase request so the bulk quote can be matched to the sample that was reviewed. If the seller says the sample and bulk versions differ, capture the difference and decide which details need confirmation before the order goes ahead. Keep the seller's wording alongside your own summary.

Hand off a shortlist someone else can use

Give the next person the original request, the entries with comparable terms, and the questions that still need answers. Include any suspected overlap between sources so they can judge how much redundancy the shortlist provides. Keep the raw messages handy for checking quantities, quoted scope, and the seller's role. A practical handoff lets a reviewer see why an offer made the cut and where they should ask for additional evidence before placing an order.
